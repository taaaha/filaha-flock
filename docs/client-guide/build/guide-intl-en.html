How Filaha Flock works, and how to get the most from it

For poultry farmers

Filaha Flock watches the air in your poultry house day and night. A sealed box fixed to the wall measures temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide (CO2) and ammonia (NH3), and keeps an eye on the mains power. When something becomes dangerous it does not wait for you to open an app: it sounds a buzzer in the house, sends you an SMS and rings your phone.

It does this over the mobile network, so it works with no internet on the farm, and it keeps working through a power cut on its own battery.

When danger appears

  1. A buzzer sounds inside the house.
  2. An SMS reaches your phone, in plain words, with the problem and the value.
  3. Your phone rings. Everything is retried until it gets through.

The app

Warranty and support
Included with the box. You can reach us by phone, WhatsApp and email.

What we need from you

  • A mains socket within reach of the box.
  • Mobile coverage at the spot, from an operator that supports the box. We confirm that your country's networks are supported before you order, and check the signal at installation.
  • A SIM with an active plan. The box uses it for data, SMS and calls; the record on the last page notes who looks after the plan.
  • A phone number that answers. Alerts go to the number we register, so tell us if it changes.

Installation

  1. The mobile signal is checked at the spot, and the place for the box and the sensors is chosen.
  2. The box is mounted on the wall and the sensors are placed at bird height.
  3. The power and the antenna are connected, and the battery is checked to be in place.
  4. Your alert phone number is registered and the app is set up on your phone, with your breed and the day the chicks arrived.
  5. A test is sent with the red button: the test SMS arriving on your phone proves the whole path works.

Your flock's age, and your alert limits

Birds need different conditions as they grow: a day-old chick needs about 33 °C, a six-week-old bird about 20 °C. Filaha Flock follows this in two parts that work together.

The app: guidance by age
Once the app knows your breed and the day the chicks arrived, it shows the right target temperature for today, announces each change of phase and each vaccination due, and judges your reports against what suits birds of that age: temperature within a few degrees of the target, humidity within ten points.
The box: limits you set
The box's alarm, SMS and call work with no internet. It starts with standard limits, and you change them by text message whenever your flock reaches a new stage; the target the app shows for today tells you where to set them. The next pages explain how.
Alert limits in the app
The colours and alerts in the app follow limits you can adjust in its settings. They are the same for every house and every age, so set them to the stage your flock is at.

How it watches, and when it warns you

About every 15 seconds the box takes its readings and sends them to our servers. A warning is raised only when a value stays in the danger zone for two readings in a row, about 30 seconds, so one stray spike never wakes you. Warnings are spaced at least 10 minutes apart.

MeasurementStandard limit: warns whenWhy it matters
Temperature38 °C or above, or 12 °C or belowHeat stress and chilling hit the flock within hours.
Humidity85 % or above, or 25 % or belowWet litter breeds disease; dry air raises dust.
CO22,500 ppm or aboveVentilation is no longer keeping up with the birds.
Ammonia (NH3)35 ppm or aboveIt burns the airways long before you can smell it.
Mains powerThe power is cutThe house is running on the box's battery.

These are the standard limits the box starts with. Change them by text message to suit your flock's age: see the next page.

What reaches you

Buzzer
Sounds in the house so anyone working there knows at once. The blue button silences it for 10 minutes.
SMS
A short message, currently in Arabic or French, written to be read in a few seconds.
Phone call
Your phone rings for about 20 seconds. Answer or not: the SMS has the details.

If the mobile network is down at that moment, the message waits and is retried for about 15 minutes until it goes through.

Setting the limits by text message

Text the box's SIM number from your registered phone. The box replies with what it saved. Change the limits as often as you like; they survive power cuts.

Send (example)It sets
T 20 32Temperature: warns below 20 °C or above 32 °C
H 40 75Humidity: warns below 40 % or above 75 %
C 2500CO2: warns at 2,500 ppm or above
N 25Ammonia (NH3): warns at 25 ppm or above
LIMITSThe box replies with the limits it is using now
RESETBack to the standard limits

You can combine them in one message: T 20 32 H 40 75 C 2500 N 25. Capitals, spaces and commas do not matter. Write with Latin letters and digits, and use a dot for decimals (32.5).

The box answers, for example: Filaha DEV01 OK: T20-32 H40-75 C2500 N25. Always check the reply: if something in your message is wrong, nothing is changed and the reply says what to fix.

Values the box accepts

Temperature
low 0–40 °C, high 10–50 °C, at least 3 °C apart
Humidity
low 0–60 %, high 40–100 %, at least 10 points apart
CO2
800–5,000 ppm
Ammonia (NH3)
5–50 ppm

Values outside these ranges are refused, so a typing slip cannot switch an alarm off. Only your registered phone number is obeyed; texts from any other number are ignored.

When to change them: when your flock reaches a new stage. Check today's target temperature in the app, then set the low and high limits a few degrees either side of it. If you are unsure which numbers to use, ask us.

A power cut

You get the same buzzer, SMS and call, and the box carries on measuring from its battery. When the mains returns you receive a confirmation SMS, roughly a minute later on purpose, so that it is never sent by mistake.

The evening report

Every evening at 8 pm the box sends one SMS with the latest readings.

If the connection drops

The box keeps measuring and stores readings for about 15 minutes, sending them when the link returns. If our servers stay silent for about 30 minutes it restarts itself. Your SMS and call alerts do not depend on the app or on internet.

Using it every day

The screen on the box shows the current readings, so you can check the house without taking out your phone.

The app

Each house appears as a live card on your phone, with current readings, history and reports. The plan you choose decides how many houses you can watch and which reports you get: Basic gives live monitoring, emergency call and SMS, and a daily report; Premium adds weekly and monthly reports, several houses and priority support.

Looking after it

  • Keep the battery connected at all times. The box needs it during power cuts, and for the strong current its radio draws when it transmits.
  • Keep the antenna connected and in place.
  • Keep the SIM plan active, and tell us if you change your alert number.
  • Wipe dust from the outside with a dry cloth. Do not spray water on the box and do not open it.
  • Press the red button once a month and check that the SMS arrives.

Getting the most from Filaha Flock

  • Put the sensors where the birds are. The box measures the air where it hangs, so its position matters.
  • Follow your flock's stage. Text new limits when your birds reach a new phase (page 5).
  • Look at the app once a day. A minute with the readings and today's target shows how the house is doing, before any alert.
  • Keep your phone ready. Alerts go to the registered number: keep it charged, with signal, and let the box's call ring through.
  • Read ammonia as a trend. Watch whether NH3 is rising, falling or steady, and set its limit to suit your flock.
  • Tell us what you see. Your feedback shapes what we build next.
